EmbodiedNet-Calibrate: Real Houses for Calibrating Simulated Ones

Chase Citrowski, Lefties Lens Limited. Companion to EmbodiedNet-Sim2Real; that paper reads the archive for layout, this one reads it for light.

Abstract

BEHAVIOR-1K calibrated its simulation to reality gap against one real apartment. Household robot learning now runs on simulated houses and on world models that generate them, and a generated interior passes for a real one at a glance. Calibrating against real houses needs real houses recorded with more range than a robot camera has. We introduce EmbodiedNet-Calibrate: a method for reading the light in any interior frame, a first reading against real houses, and the archive those houses come from. Three measures apply to any source: contrast between the window glass and the room, how unevenly light falls across each wall, and how far colour spreads across each wall where daylight meets bulb light. Read against a baseline of rooms in two houses, chosen by the operator who captured them as typical conditions, DreamDojo, BEHAVIOR and MolmoSpaces are each out of calibration in their own direction, and the direction is the useful part: a lab reads its own column and knows what to adjust.

What a glance misses

Interior video · frame · ambient light only

A real dining room, a frame of interior video from EmbodiedNet under ambient light only. The windows run brighter than the room, the bulbs and the daylight differ in colour, and the sun lands as a hard edged patch on the floor. In the generated frame it is read against in the paper, the garden through the windows is exposed like the room, one warm cast covers every surface, and soft shadow fills the room evenly.

The eye adapts across the window, balances mixed light and fills the shadow, so it accepts frames built the same way. A robot camera does none of this. A window in daylight is many times brighter than the wall beside it, bulbs and daylight mix across one wall, and light falls off quickly away from the glass. Whether a generated frame carries these is a measurement, not an impression.

Three measures of interior light

MeasureDefinition
Window contrastStops between the upper quartile of window pixels, the glass, and the median of walls, ceiling and floor. Windows below 0.5% of the frame are not counted.
Wall unevennessSpread of luminance in stops within each wall region, median over regions.
Colour spreadSpread of log(B/R) within each wall region, median over regions. One paint under one light gives a tight spread; bulbs mixed with daylight give a wide one.

Regions are labeled by a semantic segmentation model and checked frame by frame by the operator who captured the imagery; hand corrections are logged. Light sources, mirrors and tile are left out of every surface. Every measure is taken within a wall region, so a wall painted a different colour from its neighbour does not count as spread.

Windows

The merged stills hold the room's full ratio at the glass, and no window is clipped in every bracket. Every generated source sits below real video. MolmoSpaces sits furthest below: its windows are barely brighter than its walls, so its rooms are lit from everywhere at once rather than from the glass.

Walls

Each source misses in its own direction. DreamDojo's walls are far more uneven than real walls, which reads as generated texture standing in for light. MolmoSpaces walls are flatter than real. BEHAVIOR, built largely from scans of real houses, comes closest.

Colour

Real video carries the widest spread: daylight through a window meets bulb light across one wall. MolmoSpaces comes close; DreamDojo carries about half of it and BEHAVIOR about a third, so BEHAVIOR's rooms are lit by one source where real rooms are lit by two.

No source is ranked

Each row gives a direction a set builder can act on: raise the glass above the room, let one wall carry two kinds of light, keep the walls as even as paint under daylight actually is.

Interior video · frame

Shutters and reflective surfaces in a real kitchen. Each louver alternates a lit slat with a shadowed gap, and the stainless refrigerator returns streaks of window light that move with the camera.

The real reference

Two modalities carry the light. Interior stills are camera raw exposure brackets per composition, plus a single ceiling bounced flash fill frame; the brackets are merged and the flash frame left out, so the merged still records the room's own light. Interior video is shot under ambient residential light only, on a gimbal at human operating height. The baseline is three rooms in two houses, an entry with a stair and a living room in one and a primary bath in the other, captured in spring and in fall, chosen by the operator who captured every frame as typical conditions. They are a baseline, not a sample of the archive, and the same reading runs on any room in it.

Limitations: a baseline of three rooms chosen as typical, not drawn at random; generated frames are those the projects published, not frames rendered at a robot's head height, and their display conversion is not stated; window contrast in display frames depends on the conversion as well as the room; single region; marketed condition.
