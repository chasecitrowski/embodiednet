EmbodiedNet-Sim2Real: Measuring Simulated Houses Against Real Ones

Chase Citrowski, Lefties Lens Limited. Submitted to the CoRL 2026 SimBench2Real workshop.

Abstract

Simulated houses now train and evaluate most household robot policies, yet how closely they resemble real houses is rarely measured. We introduce EmbodiedNet-Sim2Real, which compares 49 real houses, drawn at random from those with metric floor plans in a single operator archive of 514 properties, with MolmoSpaces, BEHAVIOR and RoboCasa365 on six layout statistics counted under fixed rules. The simulated sets are built around the places where tasks happen and leave out much of the structure that connects them. Loops of rooms are common in real houses and rare in generated ones (47% against 0.3%), and the loops the generator does produce all pass through a bedroom. Doors that open onto closets, pantries and storage are in every sampled real house and cannot exist in the generated set. BEHAVIOR, built largely from scans of real houses, keeps rooms of real size and dead end spaces at the real rate. All three sets make kitchens larger than real ones, by 33% to 83% at the median. For each gap we note whether the set cannot represent it or simply rarely generates it. The measurement scripts for the public sets will be released, and we propose starting evaluation episodes from the robot's origin rather than in the task room.

The order a robot meets them

Levels

Nearly all real houses have more than one level, counting basements, and most have three or more. MolmoSpaces houses are single floors. BEHAVIOR keeps more than one level in most houses, but each floor loads separately, so a robot cannot move between them.

Circulation and loops

Loops are rare in MolmoSpaces and common in real houses, and every loop the generator does produce passes through a bedroom. BEHAVIOR, built from scanned real houses, has loops at a rate consistent with real ones.

Doors and dead end spaces

Every sampled real house has doors that open onto closets, pantries, storage or utility rooms. MolmoSpaces has none, so every door in it leads somewhere, and it opens every interior door by construction. A policy that learns that a door means a passage learns the wrong rule.

Rooms and kitchens

MolmoSpaces houses have about a quarter as many rooms as real houses, each nearly twice the area. Kitchens are where all three sets agree with each other and not with real houses: 33% to 83% larger at the median.

The gaps follow how a set was built. The generated set departs from real houses at every stop. The set built from scanned houses keeps loops, dead end spaces and room size, and departs in room count, in levels a robot cannot move between and in kitchen size. Layout statistics are not policy results, but where the real distribution has weight and the simulated one does not, a benchmark score says nothing about that part of real houses.

Definitions, applied identically to every set

TermDefinition
HouseOne dwelling, all floors.
RoomEvery labeled space except dead end spaces, as BEHAVIOR labels them; laundry, mud rooms and unfinished basements included. Halls, corridors, stairs, garages, open-to-below areas, outdoor areas and outbuildings are not rooms.
Room areaFloor area of every room, in m².
Kitchen areaFloor area of each room labeled kitchen, in m².
Dead end spaceA closet, pantry, storage or utility room, counted once per space. Walk-in primary closets, butler's pantries and laundry rooms are rooms, not dead end spaces.
LoopA cycle through at least one door or corridor. Rooms joined only by doorless openings are one space, so a great room is not a loop. Corridors stay in the graph.
More than one levelTwo or more floors, basement included.

All 49 real houses and all 9 BEHAVIOR houses were counted by hand under these rules by one rater, the operator who captured the imagery. MolmoSpaces statistics are computed from the metadata of all 12,000 houses in its MSProc set; all 37 houses it marks as having a loop were checked by hand against rendered plans. RoboCasa365 provides 60 kitchen layouts, so only kitchen area applies.

The real reference

Interior video · frame

A real living room, a single ungraded frame of interior video from EmbodiedNet. It opens to the kitchen and to a second level.

EmbodiedNet is a preserved sensor archive from a commercial residential media practice in Colorado: 514 properties in 239 communities, approximately 35.6 TB, collected by one operator following a documented capture method from 2020 to 2026. Of these, 267 properties have metric floor plans; the 49 real houses were drawn at random from them and average 3,434 square feet against 3,705 across the archive. This paper publishes no individual floor plan.

Two proposals for evaluation

Start from the robot's origin

Benchmark episodes usually begin in the task room. A real task begins wherever the robot is. Starting outside the task room makes every task pass through thresholds, halls, door choices and alternate routes. The change is cheap and applies to existing benchmarks.

Check against real layouts

Evaluate policies trained in simulation in reconstructions of held out real houses from the archive, and report the difference from the benchmark score. The captures are the ground truth.

Limitations: single region, marketed condition, floor plans where the engagement included them, one operator counting, printed dimensions treating every room as a rectangle. The archive records static properties, not people or physics, and layout differences are not policy results.
